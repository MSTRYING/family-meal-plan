Breakfast Warm cinnamon oat porridge
Prep 2 minCook 8 minServes 4

Thick creamy oats simmered in full-cream milk with cinnamon, topped with sliced banana and a generous drizzle of honey — the ultimate SA winter morning warmer.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups rolled oats
  • 4 cups full-cream milk
  • 1 tsp ground cinnamon
  • Pinch of salt
  • 2 bananas, sliced
  • 4 tbsp honey

Method

  1. 1Bring milk and salt to a gentle simmer in a heavy pot over medium heat.
  2. 2Stir in oats and cinnamon. Reduce heat to low.
  3. 3Cook 6–8 min, stirring frequently, until thick and creamy.
  4. 4Spoon into bowls, top with banana slices and a generous drizzle of honey.

Dinner Creamy potato & leek gratin
Prep 20 minCook 50 minServes 4

Thinly sliced potatoes and sweet leeks layered in garlic cream and topped with golden cheddar — pure, satisfying winter comfort in one dish.

CJ add-on: Grill a pork chop seasoned with salt, pepper and herbs alongside. Plate next to gratin.

Ingredients

  • 1.2kg potatoes, very thinly sliced
  • 3 leeks, cleaned and sliced
  • 300ml fresh cream
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1½ cups grated cheddar
  • 2 tbsp butter
  • Salt, pepper & nutmeg

Method

  1. 1Preheat oven to 200°C. Melt butter in a pan, sauté leeks with garlic until soft, about 5 min. Season.
  2. 2Grease a deep baking dish. Layer half the potato slices, season well.
  3. 3Spread all the leeks over the potatoes. Season again.
  4. 4Add remaining potatoes on top. Pour cream over everything. Season.
  5. 5Cover with cheddar. Cover dish with foil. Bake 35 min.
  6. 6Uncover and bake 15 min more until golden and bubbling.

BreakfastScrambled eggs on toast with wilted spinach
Prep 5 minCook 8 minServes 4

Silky, slow-scrambled eggs on buttered toast with a handful of wilted baby spinach — a protein-packed winter breakfast that keeps everyone going until lunch.

Ingredients

  • 8 large eggs
  • 4 tbsp full-cream milk
  • 2 tbsp butter
  • 100g baby spinach
  • Salt and black pepper
  • 4 slices brown bread, toasted

Method

  1. 1Whisk eggs and milk together, season well.
  2. 2Wilt spinach in a tiny splash of water in a hot pan, 30 sec. Set aside.
  3. 3Melt butter in a non-stick pan over low heat. Add eggs.
  4. 4Stir slowly and fold from the edges. Remove from heat just before fully set.
  5. 5Serve eggs and spinach on buttered toast.

DinnerButternut & red lentil dhal with basmati
Prep 15 minCook 35 minServes 4

A soul-warming dhal of red lentils and roasted butternut in a fragrant tomato-coconut base — thick, filling, and perfect over steamed basmati on a cold June night.

CJ add-on: Pan-fry a chicken thigh in oil until cooked through (about 8 min per side). Season well and serve alongside the dhal.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup red lentils, rinsed
  • 1 small butternut squash, peeled and cubed
  • 1 can coconut milk
  • 1 can chopped tomatoes
  • 1 onion, diced
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tbsp fresh ginger, grated
  • 2 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp ground turmeric
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 2 cups basmati rice
  • Salt & olive oil

Method

  1. 1Cook rice as per packet. Set aside.
  2. 2Sauté onion in oil over medium heat, 5 min. Add garlic, ginger and spices, stir 1 min.
  3. 3Add lentils, butternut, tomatoes and coconut milk. Pour in 1 cup water. Stir well.
  4. 4Simmer covered 25–30 min until lentils are soft and butternut tender. Season.
  5. 5Serve over rice. Garnish with fresh coriander if available.

Prep 10 minCook 15 minServes 4

Thick slices of bread dipped in cinnamon egg custard and pan-fried until golden, served with a quickly stewed cinnamon apple — a warming mid-week treat.

Ingredients

DinnerPasta bake with roasted butternut & sage
Prep 20 minCook 40 minServes 4

Roasted butternut and crispy sage tossed through penne with a cream and parmesan sauce, baked until golden — a hearty winter pasta bake the whole family loves.

CJ add-on: Grill a boerewors sausage or two alongside. Slice and serve next to the pasta bake.

Ingredients

  • 400g penne pasta
  • 1 butternut squash, peeled and cubed
  • 1 cup fresh cream
  • ¾ cup grated parmesan
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 10 fresh sage leaves
  • 2 tbsp butter
  • Salt, pepper, nutmeg

Method

  1. 1Preheat oven 200°C. Toss butternut in olive oil, season, roast 25 min until golden.
  2. 2Cook pasta until just al dente. Drain, reserving ½ cup pasta water.
  3. 3Melt butter in pan. Fry sage leaves until crisp, 1 min. Remove. Sauté garlic 1 min.
  4. 4Add cream, simmer 3 min. Stir in parmesan and pasta water. Season with salt, pepper, nutmeg.
  5. 5Toss pasta and butternut in sauce. Transfer to baking dish.
  6. 6Top with extra parmesan and crumble sage leaves over. Bake 15 min until golden.

DinnerChickpea & spinach curry with rice
Prep 15 minCook 30 minServes 4

A warming, spiced chickpea curry with wilted baby spinach in a thick tomato base — satisfying, wholesome, and even better the next day.

CJ add-on: Pan-fry two chicken thighs in oil until cooked through, season with salt and curry powder. Serve alongside the curry.

Ingredients

  • 200g baby spinach
  • 1 can chopped tomatoes
  • 1 can coconut milk
  • 1 large onion, diced
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tbsp grated ginger

BreakfastFluffy buttermilk pancakes with maple syrup
Prep 10 minCook 20 minServes 4

Light, pillowy American-style pancakes made with buttermilk for extra fluffiness — a Saturday morning ritual worth waking up for.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups flour
  • 2 tsp baking powder
  • ½ tsp bicarbonate of soda
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • Pinch of salt
  • 2 eggs
  • 2 cups buttermilk (or milk + 2 tbsp vinegar)
  • 3 tbsp melted butter
  • Maple syrup and sliced banana to serve

Method

  1. 1Whisk dry ingredients together. In a separate bowl whisk eggs, buttermilk and melted butter.
  2. 2Pour wet into dry. Stir until just combined — lumps are fine. Don't over-mix.
  3. 3Heat a non-stick pan over medium heat. Lightly grease.
  4. 4Pour ¼ cup batter per pancake. Cook until bubbles form and edges set, about 2 min.
  5. 5Flip. Cook 1 min more. Serve stacked with maple syrup and banana.
LunchRoasted tomato soup with grilled cheese
Prep 10 minCook 40 minServes 4

Deeply flavourful oven-roasted tomato soup blended smooth and silky, served with a crispy grilled cheese sandwich for dipping — the perfect SA winter Saturday lunch.

Ingredients

  • 1kg ripe tomatoes, halved
  • 1 onion, quartered
  • 4 garlic cloves
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • 500ml vegetable stock
  • Salt, pepper, dried oregano
  • 8 slices bread
  • Butter
  • Cheddar for grilling

Method

  1. 1Preheat oven 200°C. Arrange tomatoes, onion and garlic on a tray. Drizzle with oil. Season with salt, sugar and oregano. Roast 30 min until caramelised.
  2. 2Transfer to pot. Add stock. Simmer 5 min then blend until smooth. Season.
  3. 3Butter bread and fill with cheddar. Grill in a pan over medium heat, pressing down, until golden both sides.
  4. 4Ladle soup into bowls. Serve grilled cheese on the side for dipping.
DinnerVegetable potjie with crusty bread
Prep 20 minCook 60 minServes 4

A proper SA potjie of slow-cooked butternut, carrots, potatoes and green beans in a rich tomato and herb broth — deeply warming and made to share around a cold table.

CJ add-on: Add 500g stewing lamb to the potjie at step 3 — brown it first, then continue as normal. Or grill a separate boerewors for CJ alongside.

Ingredients

  • 1 butternut squash, cubed
  • 3 carrots, sliced
  • 4 medium potatoes, quartered
  • 200g green beans
  • 1 can chopped tomatoes
  • 1 onion, diced
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 250ml vegetable stock
  • 1 tsp each: cumin, paprika, mixed herbs
  • Salt, pepper, olive oil
  • 4 bread rolls to serve

Method

  1. 1Heat a potjie or cast-iron pot over medium heat. Sauté onion in oil until soft.
  2. 2Add garlic and spices. Stir 1 min.
  3. 3Add carrots and potatoes. Stir to coat. Add butternut.
  4. 4Pour in tomatoes and stock. Season. Cover and simmer on low heat 45 min, stirring occasionally.
  5. 5Add green beans. Cook 10 min more until all vegetables are tender.
  6. 6Serve from the pot with crusty bread rolls for mopping up the sauce.

DinnerRoasted vegetable lasagne
Prep 30 minCook 60 minServes 4

Layers of roasted courgette, pepper and butternut between rich tomato sauce and creamy béchamel, finished with bubbly mozzarella — the family's favourite Sunday supper.

CJ add-on: Brown 250g beef mince with garlic, season with salt and oregano. Add as an extra layer for CJ's portion when assembling the lasagne.

Ingredients

  • Lasagne sheets (dry, ~200g)
  • 2 courgettes, sliced
  • 2 peppers, sliced
  • ½ butternut squash, thinly sliced
  • 2 cans chopped tomatoes
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • 300g mozzarella, sliced
  • For béchamel: 50g butter, 50g flour, 600ml milk, nutmeg, salt

Method

  1. 1Preheat 200°C. Roast courgette, peppers and butternut with olive oil and salt, 25 min.
  2. 2Make tomato sauce: sauté garlic in oil, add tomatoes and oregano, simmer 15 min. Season.
  3. 3Make béchamel: melt butter, whisk in flour, cook 1 min. Gradually whisk in milk, cook until thick. Season with nutmeg and salt.
  4. 4Layer in a deep dish: tomato sauce, lasagne sheets, roasted veg, béchamel, repeat. Top with final layer of mozzarella.
  5. 5Cover with foil. Bake 30 min. Uncover, bake 15 min until golden and bubbling.
  6. 6Rest 10 min before cutting.

Nutrition guide

Making this week genuinely healthy for everyone

Vegetarian protein sources this week

Eggs

Complete protein — used 5 days

Eggs provide all 9 essential amino acids. Used in breakfast on Monday, Thursday, Sunday and folded into Wednesday's noodles. Two 18-packs cover the whole week comfortably. Rich in B12, D and choline.

Lentils

~18g protein per cup cooked

Red lentils appear in Monday's soup and Sunday's stew. High in iron, folate and fibre. Lentils are one of the best value protein sources available — the 500g bag in this plan covers both meals easily.

Chickpeas

~15g protein per cup — used 3 days

Used in Tuesday's curry, Wednesday's salad and Saturday's nachos. Also high in fibre which improves gut health and reduces insulin spikes from the rice and pasta dishes they accompany.

Kidney beans

~15g protein per cup — used 3 days

Thursday's rice bowl, Saturday's nachos and Sunday's braai sides. Excellent source of iron and slow-release carbohydrates — a great combination with rice for blood sugar stability.

Dairy and feta

B12, calcium, protein throughout

Cheese, yoghurt and feta appear in almost every day's meals. Critical for B12 intake in a vegetarian diet. The halloumi on Saturday is also a surprisingly complete protein at ~25g per 100g serving.

Peanut butter

~8g protein per 2 tbsp serving

Friday's breakfast gives a quick protein and healthy fat hit. Combined with oats (Tuesday) and eggs across the week, breakfasts are significantly more protein-rich than typical toast-based starts.

Key nutrients to watch on a vegetarian diet

Vitamin B12

Covered by dairy and eggs

This week's plan covers B12 through eggs, cheese, yoghurt and milk — all included daily. If you or your fiancée eat dairy and eggs regularly, B12 deficiency is unlikely. Consider a supplement if eggs and dairy are limited in general.

Iron

Lentils, spinach, beans — pair with vitamin C

Plant-based iron (non-haem iron) absorbs better alongside vitamin C. This plan naturally pairs iron-rich lentils and spinach with tomatoes and lemon — both high in vitamin C. This is not accidental.

Omega-3

Consider adding flaxseed or walnuts

This week's plan is low in omega-3, which is the one gap in a vegetarian diet without fish. If you have flaxseed or walnuts at home, stir them into the porridge or yoghurt breakfasts. Worth adding to next week's shop.

Fibre

Excellent — well above average

Lentils, chickpeas, kidney beans, oats and vegetables give this plan a very high fibre content. Most adults eat 15–20g/day; this plan likely hits 35–45g/day. Excellent for gut health and sustained energy levels.

Zinc

Eggs, cheese and legumes provide enough

Vegetarians sometimes run low on zinc. Eggs, cheddar, lentils and chickpeas all contribute meaningfully. Soaking dried lentils or beans before cooking (if using dried) improves zinc absorption by reducing phytates.

Calories and satiety

Well-balanced for active adults

Why Mediterranean eating works

Olive oil

Heart-healthy monounsaturated fat — used daily

Week 3 leans heavily on olive oil as the primary cooking fat. Monounsaturated fats reduce LDL cholesterol and are consistently associated with reduced cardiovascular risk. The Mediterranean diet is one of the most studied and validated dietary patterns in the world.

Legumes every day

Chickpeas, lentils, cannellini beans and black beans

Week 3 features a legume at every main meal. Legume consumption is one of the strongest dietary predictors of longevity across all studied populations. Beyond protein, they provide resistant starch that feeds beneficial gut bacteria.

Polyphenol variety

14+ different plant foods per day

Olives, tomatoes, spinach, peppers, courgettes, mushrooms and herbs contribute a wide spectrum of polyphenols — plant compounds with anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects. Variety across the week is more important than any single superfood.

Aubergine

Nasunin — a unique brain-protective antioxidant

Monday's moussaka centres on aubergine, which contains nasunin — a potent anthocyanin that protects cell membranes from oxidative damage. It's one of the few dietary sources of this specific compound. Aubergine is also very low calorie and very high in fibre.

What Week 4 adds nutritionally

Tofu

Complete plant protein — ~8g per 100g

Week 4 introduces firm tofu as a protein base in the teriyaki bowl and pad thai. Tofu is one of the few plant foods containing all 9 essential amino acids. It absorbs the flavours of whatever sauce it's cooked in — pressing it before cooking gives a satisfying crispy result.

Chia seeds

Omega-3, calcium and fibre in one spoon

Monday's chia pudding packs ~5g omega-3 ALA, 18% of the daily calcium RDA and 10g fibre per 2-tablespoon serving. Soaking overnight converts the seeds into a gel — easy to digest and very filling. One of the most nutrient-dense plant foods per gram.

Edamame

~11g complete protein per 100g

Edamame (young soybeans) in the sushi bowls provide complete protein plus isoflavones — plant compounds that support bone density and may reduce cardiovascular risk markers. One of the best plant sources of choline, important for brain health.
